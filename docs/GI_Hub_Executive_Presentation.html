Why this exists

The problem we set out to solve

  • Disconnected tools. Stock, procurement and the lining estimator each lived in separate spreadsheets — numbers never agreed.
  • No accountability. Who issued what, to whom, and who approved it was lost in email and WhatsApp.
  • Stale planning. The estimator showed quantities that didn't reflect what was actually consumed or received.
  • Manual, error-prone. Re-keying SAP codes and quantities invited mistakes that surfaced only at audit.

How it's built

System architecture

A modern three-tier stack chosen for reliability and low operating cost, shipping to the browser and as native applications from one codebase.

🖥️Presentation

A React single-page application, installable as a Windows, macOS or Android app. Offline-capable; role-aware navigation shows each user only their own workspaces.

🧠Logic

A FastAPI service holding every business rule. Permission checks live on the server, so no rule can be bypassed by reaching an address directly.

🗄️Data

PostgreSQL. Stock and estimator figures are derived from the movement history at read time, never stored — so they cannot drift.

🔌Services

On-premise AI assistant, WhatsApp Business, e-mail, PDF and Excel generation, nightly backups — each isolated and independently replaceable.

Quality gate: 1,917 automated checks guard every release — 1,228 service-level tests, 599 data-integrity checks, 57 end-to-end browser journeys and 33 estimator-arithmetic assertions, plus 1,313 cross-engine parity comparisons. No change ships unless every suite is green.

Accountability in action

Daily issue → End-of-Day commit

The control point of the whole system. Nothing touches the permanent ledger until a HOD signs off.

  • Store Keeper logs issues during the day — each line auto-stamped with who, what, when.
  • Lines sit in a pending state — reviewable, editable, rejectable.
  • HOD reviews, approves or rejects per-line, then commits End-of-Day.
  • On commit, stock is debited and the line becomes an immutable, audited consumption record.
  • Rejected lines are archived (not deleted) — the audit trail is never broken.

The rule that pays for itself

The subset rule, and strict decoupling

Two disciplines govern every estimator figure. Both were adopted after the alternative produced a confidently wrong buy list — the most expensive kind of error a planning tool can make.

Arrived is part of Ordered  —  never added to it
  • The subset rule. The quantity procured is the ceiling; the arrived quantity is the part of it already on site. Adding the two double-counted every unit on the shelf.
  • What that error cost. On real data it understated the buy list by 22,951 units across 22 of 30 materials — and on one material where the entire order had arrived, it reported nothing to buy against a real shortage of 9,685.
  • Tier segregation. Material on the shelf and material still in transit are never merged into one figure. Readiness counts only what has physically arrived.
  • Strict decoupling. Every estimator quantity comes from its own baseline. A warehouse issue does not silently move a planning number.

Real spreadsheets, not clean ones

Reading data people actually typed

The consumption workbook records a tank reference by hand, and the same vessel appears written four different ways. The system reconciles what it safely can — and refuses to guess the rest.

✅Reconciled automatically

Four spellings of one tank resolve to a single vessel. On live data that alone accounted for 58 of 103 rows that would otherwise have read as separate equipment.

⚠️Held, not guessed

One reference matched vessels on two different trains. Either answer would have looked entirely plausible in every report afterwards, so 39 rows wait for a human instead.

👤Resolved once

Someone who knows the site answers once, and every row waiting on that reference is tagged. The decision is remembered and never overwritten by a later import.

Why this matters commercially: a planning system that silently assigns consumption to the wrong equipment produces reports that are wrong and confident. Refusing to guess is the more expensive engineering choice and the only defensible one.

The step into asset management

Enterprise asset & tool tracking

Two hammers share one item code, so scanning either label used to resolve to the same inventory line — the system could not say which one was in your hand or where the other had gone. Every physical tool now has its own identity, its own position, and a history that cannot be rewritten.

🔨Serialised identity

One record per physical thing, keyed by its serial number — not per item code. Ten identical hammers are ten tracked assets.

📍GPS on scan

Scan a tool and its coordinates are captured alongside the update, becoming a map link on the record.

🕘Append-only history

Every move is kept and never overwritten, so "where has this been, and who had it" is a question with an answer.

🛠Condition & custody

Working, not in use, under repair — plus who is holding it. One field, recorded by whoever is standing in front of it.

🤝The scan resolves three ways

A serial gives you that exact tool. An item code with several units asks "which one is in your hand?". An item code with none falls back to the ordinary material view — the system never invents an asset that does not exist.

🛡️Capture never blocks the update

A declined permission, a yard with no signal, a phone without a fix — the move still records, with the coordinates simply absent. The location update is the operational value; the coordinate is the bonus.

"App wins" conflict resolution: a spreadsheet location seeds an asset on first import, but from then on what somebody recorded in the app is authoritative. A re-import cannot move a tool, revert its condition, or erase a captured position — because a person who walked to the thing and scanned it is a better source than a cell somebody typed last month.

Trust the numbers

Built for integrity

🧪1,917 automated checks

Schema, every workflow, permission lock, estimator calculation and end-to-end browser journey. Runs before every release; the build is gated on green.

🧾Immutable audit trail

Posted records are never edited in place. Corrections happen through reversals; rejected items are archived, not deleted. Asset movements are append-only.

📐Derived, not declared

Stock and estimator figures are computed from movements — eliminating the "someone typed the wrong number" class of error entirely.

🔒Fail-closed permissions

Checked centrally by request type rather than screen by screen — so a feature written next year is closed to a read-only account from the moment it exists.

Why "fail-closed" is the load-bearing word: a permission check added screen by screen is only as reliable as the developer who remembers it, and the one that gets forgotten fails open — the write succeeds and nothing looks wrong. Checking centrally inverts that. 126 of the 143 state-changing operations are blocked for a read-only account; the 17 that are not are sign-in, sign-out and a user's own security settings.

Ask a question in plain language and get the answer from this manual, with the section quoted. Retrieval is 97% faster than the previous approach and materially more accurate — it ranks by how distinctive a term is, so a rare word like a system code outweighs a common one.

Going to production

🐳Docker

The whole stack — app, AI, proxy, backups — comes up with one docker compose up. Reproducible on any Linux server.

A cost-efficient cloud server behind an Nginx reverse proxy with TLS. AI models stay on a private network — never exposed to the internet.

⚙️CI/CD

Push to GitHub → the server auto-pulls, rebuilds & restarts. Zero manual deploys, every change versioned.

💬WhatsApp Cloud API

Direct Meta Business messaging for alerts & two-way replies, with a secure webhook receiver.

🧠Private AI sidecar

FastAPI + a local LLM serve the manual assistant on-box — company data never leaves the server.

💾Automated backups

Nightly snapshots with off-box retention and a documented one-command restore path.

Memory-efficient by design: tuned to run multiple AI models on modest hardware without contention — one model resident at a time, the rest of the stack kept lean.
